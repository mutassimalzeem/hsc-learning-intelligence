# Bangladesh HSC Physics curriculum and candidate Mechanics scope

Research date: 18 Nov 2026 (Asia/Dhaka). Status: source-backed desk analysis and proposed scope; not teacher approval, an implemented content model, or evidence of product-market fit.

## Recommendation and limits

**Proposed interpretation:** retain Mechanics as a candidate domain, but start with a small motion-and-force reasoning slice rather than all Mechanics chapters. Propose **eight content nodes: four prerequisite checks and four diagnostic target concepts**, drawn from selected parts of chapters 1–4. This count follows the bounded learning task below; it is not an official curriculum count. Do not adopt the earlier 15–25-concept range as a requirement.

**Unresolved:** the pilot board, exam year, regular/irregular/improvement status, teaching language, approved textbook edition and qualified teacher review. The sources establish a curriculum baseline and a 2026 cohort distinction; they do not establish a universal examination syllabus for every future cohort. No content release or broad backend implementation is approved by this report.

## Evidence labels and method

- **Officially verified:** supported by an NCTB or education-board source below; applicability is limited to what that document states.
- **Secondary-source supported:** credible non-official evidence, identified separately. No secondary source is used to establish curriculum boundaries in this report.
- **Proposed interpretation:** educational decomposition, prerequisite edge, error opportunity, assessment design or product recommendation authored for review, not an official learning outcome or finding about students.
- **Unresolved:** evidence or qualified review is still missing.

Searches covered NCTB higher-secondary curriculum/textbook pages and board notices on full/rearranged HSC syllabuses. Official PDFs and page links were opened; direct HTTP retrieval was used where the research browser timed out. Page references below use the PDF's one-based viewer numbering; for the curriculum's subject section the printed page number is one less (viewer 31 = printed 30). Preserve both when extracting content later. Legacy-font extraction is not a clean Bangla transcription: do not copy garbled text into content records.

## Verified paper boundary and Mechanics classification

**Officially verified:** [S2, viewer p.27 and pp.30–38](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-nctb/2024/12/28d03c592c2c4b208f7782d04c78e01d.pdf#page=27) identifies these first-paper chapters, translated here: 1 Physical world/measurement; 2 Vectors; 3 Kinematics; 4 Newtonian mechanics; 5 Work/energy/power; 6 Gravitation/gravity; 7 Structural properties of matter; 8 Periodic motion; 9 Waves; 10 Ideal gas/kinetic theory.

**Proposed interpretation:** chapters 3–6 form the central particle/mechanical reasoning path, with selected chapter 1–2 prerequisites. Chapters 7–9 contain related material/oscillation/wave topics but should not silently be bundled into a first Mechanics release. Chapter 10 is a separate candidate domain. This is our scope classification, not an NCTB unit called “Mechanics.”

## MVP suitability and alternatives

All judgments in this section are **Proposed interpretation**, not measured learner need or empirical learning-effect findings.

| Criterion | Motion/force slice assessment | Trade-off and required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coverage/coherence | A linked path across selected early chapters can support one explain/repair/verify loop | It covers only parts of chapters; never advertise complete Mechanics or exam preparation |
| Dependencies | Units, directions and motion graphs expose prerequisite failures before force inference | A wrong force response may be mathematical or linguistic; prerequisite checks must not become misconception certainty |
| Diagnostic opportunity | Paired contexts can test whether reasoning follows acceleration rather than motion direction | Different wrong beliefs can produce the same option; require reasoning/follow-up and allow abstention |
| Teacher-reviewable items | Short diagrams and controlled changes can make reviewer discussion concrete | Physics correctness, system boundary, wording and distractors still need independent review; no question bank is approved |
| Small pilot | A bounded slice can compare one reviewed intervention with independent transfer tasks | Cohort size/access/consent and prior topic exposure are unconfirmed; no causal inference from an uncontrolled pilot |
| Learning improvement | Immediate transfer and delayed new contexts could reveal improvement beyond answer repetition | This is a measurable hypothesis, not a promised outcome; baseline and missing follow-up must be recorded |

**Proposed interpretation:** measurement alone is a smaller alternative with fewer conceptual dependencies, but might demonstrate correction of procedural/unit errors more readily than a rich concept-gap explanation. Vectors alone isolates representational reasoning but risks becoming a mathematics exercise. Ideal gases offers controlled-variable tasks yet introduces microscopic-model assumptions. Periodic motion/waves can yield useful contrasts but add phase and oscillatory-representation demands. These are candidate trade-offs grounded in the mapped topics, not a ranking of student difficulty or market demand.

Recommend motion/force conditionally: proceed to teacher review, not production authoring. Narrow to prerequisites if force explanations remain ambiguous; reconsider another first-paper domain if reviewer evidence or pilot access favors it.

## Bounded candidate: eight proposed content nodes

**Proposed interpretation:** P1–P4 are prerequisite checks; T1–T4 are the initial diagnostic targets. IDs below are local research labels, not accepted content-model identifiers. A node may need splitting after review; eight is a planning starting point, not a mandated total.

| Node | Proposed learning objective | Proposed prerequisite edges | Official curriculum anchor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 Units and quantities | Check that a numerical response uses compatible units before interpreting its physical meaning | Arithmetic and simple ratios | S2 viewer p.30, ch.1 outcome 6 |
| P2 Directed quantities | Represent one-axis quantities with signed arrows; add opposite-direction components | P1 and a stated axis | S2 viewer p.31, ch.2 outcomes 1–5 |
| P3 Motion quantities | Separate position, velocity and change in velocity in a short narrated sequence | P1, P2 | S2 viewer p.31, ch.3 outcomes 2–3 |
| P4 Motion graphs | Infer constant/nonconstant velocity and acceleration sign from simple graphs | P3, basic gradient | S2 viewer p.31, ch.3 outcome 3 |
| T1 Object and interactions | Choose the body of interest and identify forces acting on it; use a force diagram as an instructional representation | P2; teacher-reviewed idealizations | S2 viewer p.32, ch.4 outcomes 1, 4; the diagram decomposition is proposed |
| T2 Resultant and inertia | Distinguish zero resultant from no forces and from zero velocity | P3, P4, T1 | S2 viewer p.32, ch.4 outcomes 3–4 |
| T3 Force and acceleration | Compare acceleration direction/magnitude for a constant-mass body under a stated resultant | P1–P4, T1, T2; proportionality | S2 viewer p.32, ch.4 outcomes 2, 4; constant-mass restriction is proposed |
| T4 Interaction pairs | Identify forces on different bodies and distinguish them from balancing forces on one body | P2, T1, T2 | S2 viewer p.32, ch.4 outcomes 3–4, 9; does not cover the momentum-conservation part of outcome 9 |

All node anchors refer to the [official curriculum PDF](https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-nctb/2024/12/28d03c592c2c4b208f7782d04c78e01d.pdf#page=30). Proposed sequencing: P1 → P2 → P3 → P4; P2 → T1; P3/P4/T1 → T2; T2 → T3; T1/T2 → T4. These are instructional dependencies for review, not a claim that NCTB mandates this sequence.

### Explicit exclusions from the first slice

**Proposed interpretation:** exclude full chapter completion; calculus derivations and vector operators; oblique/projectile and circular-motion problem sets; momentum/collisions, rotational dynamics and torque; work/energy/power; orbital/field calculations; materials/fluids; oscillations/waves; gas theory; practical-laboratory certification and examination-format emulation. Straight-line constant-mass idealizations should be stated in each item. Do not add friction/inclines or complicated lift/contact systems without separate source mapping and review of the chosen textbook.

**Officially verified:** S2 includes calculus in its vector/kinematics and second-law outcomes (viewer pp.31–32). **Proposed interpretation:** an introductory qualitative/constant-mass slice may omit those derivations only if clearly labeled partial; it cannot be described as satisfying the complete official outcomes.

### Assessment and content-model handoff

**Proposed interpretation:** for each target, draft contrasting contexts plus reasoning/follow-up prompts, a reviewed repair explanation, an independent transfer task and a delayed variant. Do not reuse the same item as evidence of learning improvement. Set item count from evidence needs and reviewer capacity rather than adopting 50–100 as a quota. Separate misconception hypotheses from mathematical/reading slips and guessing. No wrong option uniquely establishes a misconception, and no single correct transfer response establishes mastery.

Before implementation, prepare a review pack in which each proposed node/item retains: curriculum authority/title/version; paper/chapter/outcome and PDF viewer/printed page; applicable exam year/candidate status; textbook author/publisher/edition/approval evidence and rights; proposed objective and edge rationale; item/version/key/distractor rationale; supporting/disconfirming evidence; reviewer status/date and unresolved alternatives. These are documentation requirements to inform a later content-model review, not a new schema or API design.
