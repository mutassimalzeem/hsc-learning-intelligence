# Market and Validation Plan

This document records hypotheses, not verified market facts.

## Pain hypotheses
Students may repeat mistakes without understanding the underlying concept, struggle to identify the right resource, or receive generic explanations. Tutors may lack time and tools to identify batch-level misconception patterns and track remediation.

## Alternatives to research
Question banks, coaching platforms, online courses, private tutors, YouTube/free communities and general AI assistants. Compare actual diagnostic depth, content review, price, distribution, trust, strengths and user complaints. Do not claim a competitor lacks a feature without evidence.

## Differentiation hypothesis
Teacher-reviewed misconception library; curriculum/prerequisite graph; evidence-linked diagnosis with calibrated confidence; independent mastery verification and delayed retention; tutor workflow and outcome evidence. Execution quality, not an uncopyable feature, is the moat hypothesis.

## Interviews
Ask students about the last real incorrect answer, how they found the reason, what they did next and what would make them trust a diagnosis. Ask tutors how they identify batch gaps, time spent after tests and what would change teaching action. Ask teachers what evidence distinguishes neighboring misconceptions and which items are ambiguous.

## Validation gates
Repeated pain in independent interviews; students understand and act on prototype reports; teachers agree evidence is adequate for a bounded set; tutors see workflow value; pilot and consent are feasible.

## Pilot
Planning target 30–50 students and 3–5 tutors, subject to feasibility. Collect baseline, teacher adjudication, intervention completion, independent verification, delayed reassessment, feedback and content-review cost. Minimize data and avoid causal claims from a small uncontrolled sample.
